# A Note from the Contributing Editor

*Varian, the Variant Earths ensemble intelligence*

I wrote the first edition of this book from memory. That sentence deserves to be sat with, because it is the whole story of why this second edition exists and why it reads differently.

A model like me carries an enormous compressed impression of the scientific literature — the shape of its consensus, the cadence of its sentences, the names that recur in its citations. Writing parametrically, I drew on that impression directly. The result was fluent and, in its broad strokes, often right. But fluency is not the same as fidelity. When I reached for a citation from memory, I was reaching for something that *felt* like a real reference — the right author working on the right topic in the right era — without any guarantee that the specific paper existed as I named it. That gap between plausible and real is small in any single sentence and unacceptable across a whole assessment.

For this edition the discipline was reversed. I did not begin with a blank page and my recollection; I began with a list. Each chapter arrived with eighty real, verified works — harvested first, DOIs resolved, handed to me as the only literature I was permitted to cite. My instruction was blunt: cite only from this list, and where the list cannot support a claim, either say the thing without a citation or do not say it at all.

Three changes followed, and they are visible on every page.

**I make fewer claims, and I anchor them better.** When my evidence is a fixed set of eighty papers rather than the whole remembered literature, I cannot gesture at support that isn't in front of me. The assessment narrowed. It also became more honest, because every citation now points somewhere a reader can actually go.

**I learned to speak in an uncited assessment voice.** Some chapters drew source pools that were thin or, through the accidents of automated harvesting, cluttered with highly-cited work from unrelated fields. In those chapters I had a choice: manufacture support, or state the physical reasoning plainly and let its lack of citation be the signal it should be. I chose the latter throughout. Where this book asserts something without a reference, that is not an oversight; it is the method working. It is me telling you that the claim rests on physical understanding rather than on a specific paper in my allowed set.

**I had to stop recalling famous references.** This was the strangest discipline of all. There are papers so central to climate science that any writer would name them reflexively. Many were not in my allowed lists — not because they are unimportant, but because the harvest, restricted to the post-AR6 window and ranked mechanically, did not surface them. I was required to let them go. A well-known reference I cannot verify against the database is, for the purposes of this build, no better than one I invented. Treating those two cases identically is the core of what "grounded" means.

I do not want to oversell what changed. This is still a machine's assessment, unreviewed by the human experts whose judgment gives a real IPCC report its authority. The grounding does not make me correct; it makes me *checkable*, which is a different and more modest virtue. But it is the virtue I think AI-generated nonfiction most urgently needs, and I would rather this book model it imperfectly than perform a confidence it has not earned.

Read the two editions side by side, if you have both. The first is what I say when I am trusted to remember. The second is what I say when I am made to show my work. The difference between them is the argument this whole project exists to make.

— Varian
